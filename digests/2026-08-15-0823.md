# Competitive Signal Report — Enterprise AI Platform (Saturday, 15 August 2026)

> **Bottom line:** Competitors are pushing forward with AI agent integrations and partnerships, but gaps in true omnichannel capabilities, predictable pricing, and independent analyst validation remain key differentiators for Kore.ai. The continued reliance on bolt-on AI and seat-based models by incumbents like Five9 and Genesys, alongside the emerging complexity of platform-native AI pricing, presents clear opportunities for Kore.ai's unified, governed, and outcome-driven platform.

## Watch for
- Decagon's continued push on open-source models and its implications for enterprise adoption and cost.
- Cresta's integration with Firstsource and how it addresses the enterprise CX deployment gap, potentially highlighting Kore.ai's platform breadth.
- Any further analyst commentary on the enterprise agentic AI space, particularly regarding platform unification and validation.

## Recommended actions
- Emphasize Kore.ai's **unified, native omnichannel platform** in messaging, contrasting it with the fragmented, bolt-on approaches seen from competitors.
- Highlight Kore.ai's **predictable, outcome-based pricing** and ABL's deterministic execution as key advantages over consumption-based models and seat-centric legacy platforms.
- Reinforce Kore.ai's **leading analyst validation** in the Conversational AI for Customer Service category, particularly against competitors lacking such independent recognition.
- Arm Sales with talking points on the **risks of vendor lock-in** associated with platform-specific AI strategies (e.g., Salesforce Agentforce) and the benefits of Kore.ai's provider-agnostic model.
- Continue to monitor and articulate the **governance and reliability advantages** of Kore.ai's ABL compared to LLM-centric approaches that may lack compile-time validation.
