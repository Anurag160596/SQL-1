# Slide — Kore.ai Artemis vs ElevenLabs

**Eyebrow:** STRATEGIC POSITIONING ▪ KORE.AI VS ELEVENLABS
**Title:** Build agents that **delegate, adapt, and know who is asking**
**Strategic frame:** Enterprises need agents that split work across systems, spend the right money
on the right turn, and answer only what the caller is entitled to hear.

Three axes. Orchestration. Model architecture. Knowledge control.

---

## LEFT COLUMN — Kore.ai
`ENTERPRISE-GRADE, AI-NATIVE PLATFORM`

**01 · Agents delegate and work in parallel**
Six orchestration primitives, including supervisor, delegation and fan-out. A supervisor splits one
request across agents, keeps control, and assembles the answer.
`GARTNER` `FORRESTER`

**02 · The right model for each turn**
Agents declare tiers, not vendors. Fast models take the simple 80%, reasoning models the complex
20%, and the platform routes around a provider outage.
`EVEREST`

**03 · Answers scoped to the caller**
Permission-scoped retrieval with citations. The agent returns only what this customer is entitled
to see, and shows where it came from.
`EVEREST` `FORRESTER`

---

## RIGHT COLUMN — ElevenLabs
`VOICE PLATFORM`

**01 · Agents hand off and cannot come back**
The engine "does not support traditional parallel execution or supervisor delegation with returns."
A transfer is "a conversation takeover, not a delegated subtask."
`PRODUCT DOCS`

**02 · One model for every turn**
The model is fixed per agent, with no routing by cost or complexity and no fallback. LLM cost is
billed on top of the per-minute rate, so a balance check is priced like a dispute.
`PRODUCT DOCS` `PRICING`

**03 · Access control stops at the agent**
Knowledge permissions are workspace-level, siloed per agent. The control is which agent sees which
document, not which caller is entitled to which answer.
`PRODUCT DOCS`
